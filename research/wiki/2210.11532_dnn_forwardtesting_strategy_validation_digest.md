## 2. Summary

1. **Motivation.** Traders typically validate algorithmic strategies via **backtesting** — applying candidate technical indicators to historical data and picking whichever produced the highest past profit — then deploy the winning strategy going forward. The authors argue this is flawed because the future need not resemble the past, and propose **DNN-forwardtesting**: instead of picking the strategy that worked best on the past, pick the strategy that would work best on a **DNN-predicted possible future**, exploiting a deep neural network's ability to learn nonlinear patterns that classic indicator-based backtesting cannot capture.

2. **Dataset construction.** The authors began with **ten randomly selected NYSE/Nasdaq securities** (CSGKF, EOG, META, NKE, DIS, PG, QQQ, IBM, ANF, CS), using daily **OHLC** price data from **30 October 2011 to 30 November 2021** (2,537 open market days), sourced from Yahoo Finance.

3. **Volatility-based stock selection via k-means.** To find comparable, "medium-volatility" assets, the team computed four **Historical Volatility (HV) estimators** — **Parkinson (PK), Garman-Klass (GK), Rogers-Satchell (RS), and Yang-Zhang (YZ)** — for all ten stocks, standardized the data, and clustered it with **K-means++** (optimal **k=6** found via the Elbow method). **ANF (Abercrombie & Fitch)** and **EOG (EOG Resources)** were selected because their volatility observations spread across all six clusters for every estimator, making them representative test assets from unrelated sectors.

4. **Independence verification.** To confirm ANF and EOG were suitable, uncorrelated assets for generalization, the authors computed the **Pearson correlation coefficient (0.28 overall)** and applied **Dynamic Time Warping (DTW)** (minimum path cost **d = 209.95**) between the two close-price series, concluding the stocks are "almost completely uncorrelated" and hence a good generalization test.

5. **Statistical forecasting baselines underperform.** Two classical forecasting methods were benchmarked as baselines: **Auto-ARIMA** (via the `pmdarima` Python library, selecting orders by minimizing AIC) and **Facebook Prophet** (used out-of-the-box). ARIMA's best models were **ARIMA(0,1,1)** for ANF (AIC 7294.347) and **ARIMA(0,1,0)**, i.e. a random walk, for EOG (AIC 10369.768); error metrics were poor (e.g., ANF: **MSE 25.49, MAPE 0.09**; EOG: **MSE 56.23, MAPE 0.06**). Prophet performed even worse on EOG (**MSE 713.61, MAPE 0.30**), especially after 2020 when volatility rose, confirming neither classical technique is adequate for this forecasting task.

6. **Deep neural network forecasting outperforms baselines.** Four feed-forward DNNs (one per OHLC component) were trained with an input window of the **previous 5 days** to forecast the **next 30 days**, using an architecture of two hidden layers (**10*t and 5*t neurons**), **dropout 0.2%**, **ReLU** activations, **L1 loss (MAE)**, and the **Adam** optimizer; hyperparameters were tuned via **scikit-learn's GridSearchCV**. The DNN dramatically beat both statistical baselines on close-price prediction (ANF: **MSE 1.75, MAPE 0.02, EVS 0.91**; EOG: **MSE 2.39, MAPE 0.01, EVS 0.7**).

7. **DNN-forwardtesting trading experiment.** Using the DNN-forecasted 30-day future, the system tested ten common technical indicators (SMA, EMA, MACD, Bollinger Bands, Stochastic, William %R, Momentum, RSI, ATR, Price Oscillator) plus TEMA, ADX, and combinations, to pick the most profitable one per stock. **Triple Exponential Moving Average (TEMA)** was selected for ANF and **Average Directional Index (ADX)** for EOG. Applying these strategies to the real future 30-day period (starting from a **$100** budget, compounded) yielded: ANF — **3 trades, $6.126 total return, Expectancy 2.112, Sharpe 2.194, Sortino 3.340, Calmar 12.403**; EOG — **3 trades, $1.374 total return, Expectancy 0.525, Sharpe 1.253, Sortino 2.556, Calmar 5.814**.

8. **Baseline (traditional backtesting) comparison.** Applying the same ten indicators via traditional backtesting on the 30 days *before* the test period, ADX was again chosen for EOG (same result as DNN-forwardtesting), but for ANF the traditionally backtested choice was **RSI** (period 5, overbought=70, oversold=30) instead of TEMA. When this RSI-based strategy was actually applied to the real future 30 days, it produced a **loss of -$1.168** (Expectancy **-1.1683**, Sharpe 0.119, Sortino 0.158, Calmar **-0.935**) versus the **$8.741** it had earned on the past 30 days — demonstrating that a strategy chosen by classic backtesting can be far less profitable (or even lose money) going forward compared to the DNN-forwardtesting-selected strategy.

9. **Conclusion and stated limitations.** The results confirm that DNN-forwardtesting selects a strategy that is equal to or more profitable than traditional backtesting when validated on real future data, indicating that DNN forecasts encode deeper trend information than simple indicator-based backtesting can. The study is limited to only **two assets (ANF and EOG)** with **medium volatility**, a single 30-day forward-test window, and ten/twelve technical indicators; the authors explicitly flag DNNs as "black-box" decision systems raising ethical/explainability concerns, and note the approach still needs testing on other markets (e.g., cryptocurrencies, DeFi tokens) and other timeframes (day trading, scalping).

10. **Future work.** The authors plan to extend the dataset with proper feature selection and class balancing (buy/sell/hold) using their prior **Generative 1 Nearest Neighbours** resampling algorithm and targeted oversampling techniques, and to study DNN trading decisions through a Logic-based Machine Learning / Multi-Agent ethical-monitoring lens in future papers.

## 3. Key Importance Topics

### DNN-forwardtesting as a strategy-selection paradigm
Rather than replacing backtesting's data (still historical), the paper changes *what* the candidate strategies are evaluated against: a DNN-predicted synthetic future rather than the raw historical past. The best-performing technical indicator is chosen by simulating trades on this predicted future, then that same choice is applied to the real future for validation.

**Why it matters:** This reframes strategy validation as a forecasting-guided selection problem instead of a pure historical-optimization problem, directly addressing the classic overfitting/curve-fitting risk of standard backtesting where the "best" past strategy often fails going forward.

### Volatility-based asset clustering (K-means++ on HV estimators)
The paper uses four distinct historical volatility estimators (Parkinson, Garman-Klass, Rogers-Satchell, Yang-Zhang) as clustering features, applying K-means++ with the Elbow method to group ten stocks by volatility profile, then deliberately picks two "medium volatility" stocks from unrelated sectors to avoid cherry-picking easy, obviously-profitable assets like Tesla or Apple.

**Why it matters:** This is a disciplined, reproducible method for selecting representative test assets in financial ML research, reducing the risk that results are an artifact of choosing convenient trending stocks, and it demonstrates a rigorous way to quantify "volatility regime" beyond simple standard deviation.

### DNN forecasting architecture and training recipe
The DNN setup (5-day lookback window, 2 hidden layers sized 10*t/5*t, dropout 0.2%, ReLU, L1/MAE loss, Adam optimizer, GridSearchCV hyperparameter tuning) is a concrete, lightweight feed-forward design that substantially outperformed ARIMA and Prophet on OHLC price forecasting.

**Why it matters:** It shows that even a relatively simple DNN (not an LSTM/Transformer) can capture nonlinear structure in price series that classical statistical models miss, making it a practical, low-complexity baseline for retail-scale forecasting projects.

### Statistical vs. deep learning forecasting benchmark
The paper directly benchmarks Auto-ARIMA and Facebook Prophet against the DNN on identical data and identical error metrics (MSE, RMSE, MAE, MAPE, EVS), giving an apples-to-apples comparison rather than citing prior literature secondhand.

**Why it matters:** Concrete numeric benchmarking (e.g., DNN MAPE of 0.01–0.02 vs. ARIMA/Prophet's 0.06–0.30) gives practitioners quantitative evidence for when to prefer neural forecasting over classical time-series models in financial contexts with non-stationary, heteroscedastic data.

### Risk-adjusted profit metrics for trading validation
Beyond raw return, the paper evaluates strategies using Expectancy Ratio, Sharpe Ratio, Sortino Ratio, and Calmar Ratio, and shows a case (ANF RSI strategy) where a strategy profitable in the past becomes an outright loss in the real future.

**Why it matters:** This illustrates precisely why single-metric backtesting (total return only) is dangerously insufficient, and models the kind of multi-metric, forward-looking risk assessment that should be standard practice before deploying any live trading strategy.

## 4. Ideas for Development

- **Forward-testing strategy validation** — Apply the DNN-forwardtesting concept (evaluate candidate strategies against a model-predicted future rather than only the historical past) to any quant strategy pipeline as an additional validation gate before live deployment, reducing reliance on potentially overfit backtests.
- **Volatility-based asset clustering for universe selection** — Reuse the K-means++ clustering on Parkinson/Garman-Klass/Rogers-Satchell/Yang-Zhang volatility estimators to systematically bucket a larger trading universe (e.g., an S&P 500 subset or crypto assets) into volatility regimes, then select test/training assets deliberately rather than by convenience.
- **DTW/Pearson independence checks for dataset design** — Use the paper's Pearson-correlation + Dynamic Time Warping combination to verify that assets chosen for a multi-asset backtest or portfolio study are genuinely independent, avoiding data leakage or spurious generalization claims from correlated instruments.
- **Lightweight DNN forecasting baseline** — Adopt the paper's simple two-hidden-layer feed-forward network (5-day lookback, dropout, ReLU, Adam, L1 loss) as a fast, low-compute forecasting baseline before investing in heavier architectures (LSTM, Transformer) for a new price-prediction task.
- **Multi-metric risk-adjusted comparison harness** — Build a standard evaluation harness that always reports Expectancy, Sharpe, Sortino, and Calmar ratios (not just total return) when comparing any two trading strategy selection methods, following the paper's Table 8/Table 9 comparison format.
- **Indicator ensemble search over predicted vs. historical windows** — Generalize the paper's indicator search (SMA, EMA, MACD, Bollinger Bands, Stochastic, W%R, Momentum, RSI, ATR, Price Oscillator, TEMA, ADX and combinations) into an automated sweep that can be re-run against any forecast source (statistical or neural) to select entry/exit rules.
