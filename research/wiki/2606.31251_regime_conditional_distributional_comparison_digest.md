## 2. Summary

1. **Problem.** Conventional strategy evaluation reduces each performance metric to **a single number over the full backtest horizon** — one Sharpe ratio, one information ratio — discarding all information about how performance varies with market conditions and how dispersed it is across evaluation windows. Two strategies with identical aggregate metrics can differ sharply in their distributional characteristics, and scalar point-estimate tests are blind to this.

2. **Proposed solution.** Model the **full conditional distribution** of a per-fold performance measure as a function of observable market-regime covariates, then compare strategies regime by regime instead of with one aggregate number.

3. **Backtest setup.** A **walk-forward backtest** on daily S&P 500 closing prices (**2 Jan 2002 – 31 Dec 2025**): 160-day in-sample training window, 40-day out-of-sample window, non-overlapping 40-day steps → **146 folds**. Active strategy: a **polynomial-kernel SVM (SVMP)** (regression mode, c=2, d=2) trained on **seven lagged technical indicators** (SMA deviation, MACD signal, Stochastic fastK/fastD/slowD, RSI, Williams %R), min-max normalized to [−1,1] on the in-sample window only. Signals are three-class: **long** above the 60th percentile of fitted in-sample values, **short** below the 40th, **cash** in between. Benchmark: **buy-and-hold (BH)**.

4. **Performance measure.** Per-fold **Adjusted Information Ratio (IR\*)**: squared, zero-floored mean return over volatility × maximum drawdown. IR\* is non-negative with a **point mass at exactly zero** — **26.7%** of BH folds and **47.9%** of SVMP folds score exactly 0.

5. **Statistical model.** That mixed discrete-continuous structure motivates a **GAMLSS** (Generalised Additive Model for Location, Scale and Shape) with a **Zero-Adjusted Gamma (ZAGA)** response — point mass ν at zero plus a Gamma distribution for the positive part. All three parameters (**μ, σ, ν**) are conditioned simultaneously on two own-series regime covariates — **realised volatility (RV)** and **cumulative 40-day momentum (MOM)** — plus a strategy dummy D and its interactions. The pooled model (n = 292) is selected by stepwise **GAIC/BIC**; independent per-parameter selection wins with GAIC **−1430.30** vs −1421.08 (constrained) and −1137.74 (null).

6. **Comparison machinery.** (i) Analytical regime-specific gaps in expected IR\* (**ΔE**) and its variance (**ΔVar**), from the ZAGA moments E(Y) = (1−ν)μ and Var(Y) = (1−ν)μ²(σ²+ν); (ii) a **parametric bootstrap** (9,999 replications) testing three null hypotheses — on E(IR\*), Var(IR\*), and their signal-to-noise ratio — at **six quantile-matched regime points** (Min, 1st Qu., Median, Mean, 3rd Qu., Max of MOM/RV).

7. **Headline result: momentum-conditioned dominance reversal.** Under **low/negative momentum** (Min → Mean regimes), **SVMP beats BH** in expected IR\* (largest gap **ΔE = +0.011** at Min; bootstrap p̂₁ = 1.000 — not one of 9,999 replications had BH ahead). Under **strong positive momentum** the sign flips: at Max, **BH dominates massively** (**ΔE = −0.022**, p̂₁ = 0.000). Neither strategy dominates globally — dominance is strictly regime-dependent.

8. **Variance verdict.** SVMP carries **structurally higher IR\* variance** across almost all regimes, and on the signal-to-noise ratio **SVMP's variance penalty swamps its return advantage**: BH achieves the superior risk-adjusted ratio from the Median regime onward (p̂₃ = 0.000).

9. **Limitations.** One index, one historical period, one ML model class; IR\* computed **gross of transaction costs**; inference depends on the ZAGA assumption; **near-complete separation** in the ν-equation at the most adverse regime (MOM perfectly predicts zero-IR\* folds for SVMP) inflates those coefficients and makes the signal-to-noise test undefined at the Min regime.

## 3. Key Importance Topics

### Distributional strategy comparison instead of scalar metrics
The paper replaces "Strategy A has Sharpe 1.2, Strategy B has Sharpe 0.9" with a **full conditional distribution** of the performance measure per market regime, and tests hypotheses about mean, variance, and signal-to-noise separately.

**Why it matters:** Single-number backtest comparisons routinely pick strategies that **win on average but lose exactly when it hurts** (e.g., in high-volatility bear regimes). Modeling the distribution makes regime-dependent fragility visible and statistically testable before capital is committed.

### Zero-Adjusted Gamma (ZAGA) for performance measures with mass at zero
IR\* lands at exactly zero in **27–48% of folds**. A plain continuous distribution cannot represent that; ZAGA combines a Bernoulli zero-mass with a Gamma positive tail, and the zero-probability ν itself becomes a modeled, regime-dependent quantity.

**Why it matters:** Many practical trading metrics (truncated ratios, hit-rate-conditioned returns, per-window profit after a floor) have this **mixed structure**. Modeling the point mass explicitly turns "how often does the strategy do nothing/fail" into a first-class, covariate-driven output.

### Momentum-conditioned dominance reversal between ML strategy and buy-and-hold
SVMP beats BH in flat/negative momentum; BH crushes SVMP in strongly trending markets. The crossover appears in **opposite-signed interaction coefficients** (D·MOM = −57.4 vs MOM = +53.8 in the μ-equation).

**Why it matters:** Direct, quantified evidence that an **active ML strategy's edge is a regime phenomenon**, not a constant. It argues for regime-conditional strategy *selection* (hold BH in strong trends, deploy the active model in choppy/bear regimes) rather than picking one winner for all conditions.

### Variance penalty can swamp mean advantage
Even where SVMP's expected IR\* is higher, the bootstrap signal-to-noise test shows **BH's risk-adjusted consistency is superior** from the median regime onward, because SVMP's IR\* variance is an order of magnitude larger.

**Why it matters:** **Mean-only comparisons systematically flatter high-variance strategies.** Testing E, Var, and E/√Var as three separate hypotheses reveals when a "better on average" strategy is actually a worse bet per unit of performance uncertainty.

### Parametric bootstrap from a fitted conditional distribution
Instead of permutation tests that treat all folds as exchangeable, synthetic IR\* samples are drawn from the fitted **ZAGA(μ̂, σ̂, ν̂) at chosen regime points**, preserving the regime conditioning in the null distribution.

**Why it matters:** It gives **calibrated p-values at specific market states** ("does the strategy beat the benchmark at median volatility and negative momentum?") — the question a portfolio manager actually asks — and the same machinery doubles as a scenario/stress-testing simulator.

### Interpretability by construction (XAI angle)
With linear regime predictors, each covariate's contribution to each distributional parameter is **directly readable from the coefficient table** — no post-hoc explanation layer needed.

**Why it matters:** Strategy-evaluation frameworks increasingly face **model-risk and governance scrutiny**. A fully parametric, coefficient-readable evaluation model is auditable in a way black-box performance attribution is not.

## 4. Ideas for Development

- **Regime-switching strategy allocator** — Use the fitted ΔE(MOM, RV) surface prospectively: classify the current regime in real time from rolling realised volatility and momentum, and **allocate between the active strategy and passive benchmark** based on which dominates in that regime. The paper explicitly flags this as an extension but does not build it.
- **Apply ZAGA/GAMLSS to any zero-inflated trading metric** — Per-trade profit after costs, per-window drawdown-adjusted return, or strategy hit-rate all exhibit point masses; the same **fitDist → zero-adjusted-family workflow** applies directly, independent of this paper's SVMP setup.
- **Stress-testing via the fitted conditional distribution** — Feed **extreme (MOM, RV) values** through a fitted GAMLSS and draw synthetic performance samples to answer "what does my strategy's performance distribution look like in a 2008-style regime" without needing that regime in the backtest window.
- **Distributional walk-forward reporting** — Replace the single aggregate Sharpe in any existing backtest report with **per-fold metric sequences plus a six-regime ΔE/ΔVar table**; a drop-in reporting upgrade requiring no change to the strategy itself.
- **Transaction costs as distributional covariates** — Let estimated costs and bid-ask spreads **enter the μ/σ/ν equations as covariates**, so net-of-cost performance distributions become regime- and friction-conditional.
- **Richer regime covariates** — Swap or augment RV/MOM with **macro-financial indicators, sentiment indices, or hidden-Markov latent states**; the GAMLSS structure accepts any covariate set without changing the comparison framework.
