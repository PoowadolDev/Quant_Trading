## What the build found

### Completion falls as the entry threshold rises

`outcomes.py` on `XLP~XLB`, twenty-eight years:

| entry z | trades | reached the exit | predicted move | realised per trade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.5 | 134 | 31% | 269 | +44.1 |
| 2.0 | 75 | 27% | 404 | +40.3 |
| 2.5 | 30 | 17% | 539 | −80.3 |
| 3.0 | 11 | 18% | 673 | −96.8 |

The formula predicts more from a wider entry, because the distance from `entry_z` back to
`exit_z` is longer. The trades do the opposite. What rises with z is not the size of the
reversion but the chance there is none left to catch: at z = 3.0, nine of eleven trades
ended at the stop.

**A large deviation is evidence the relationship has broken. The pipeline has been reading
it as evidence of opportunity.** That is the mechanism behind the 10× to 68× gap found
earlier, stated precisely rather than inferred.

### The modelled entry floor loses money

`thresholds.py` first inverted the cost arithmetic and reported the z below which a trade
cannot pay for its own financing. On `XLP~XLB` that is z = 0.61. Traded:

```
entry 0.61   339 trades   gross +4,124   carry -3,609   net  -501
entry 1.50   116 trades   gross +5,803   carry -2,041   net +3,414
```

The floor was right about what it computed and wrong about what it implied. It charges
financing for the fitted half-life and credits a move the trade only collects on
completion — and 27% of them complete.

So the script now reads the floor off trades that were actually placed. It replays the
strategy across a grid of thresholds and takes the **lowest** one whose own trades earned
`--min-edge` times their own cost, printing the modelled floor beside it. Lowest rather
than best: picking the most profitable cell of a swept grid is the behaviour Step 4 exists
to catch, and a boundary moves less than a maximum.

That measured floor is still in-sample to the grid it was read off. It needs the same
unseen-window treatment that killed `XLP~XLB` before +3,414 means anything.

### Sizing rejects the candidate without being told to

On net returns `XLP~XLB` has a mean of +23.2 bps per trade, a per-trade deviation of 309.5,
and 75 trades — a standard error of 35.7. One standard error below the estimate is −12.6.
Seventy-five trades do not establish that the mean is positive, so the growth-optimal size
is zero. That conclusion is reached from the trade returns alone, with no reference to the
cointegration or hedge-stability evidence that reached the same place.

## Verification

164 checks, in ten groups. The ones that carry weight:

- **the OU round trip** — a simulated process with a chosen theta, mu and sigma, where
  completion rate and payoff follow from theory. The prediction holds within 3× there. That
  is what makes the same script's verdict on real data worth reading: it fails on `XLP~XLB`
  because `XLP~XLB` is not an OU process, not because the script cannot measure one.
- **the financing inversion** — thresholds worked out on paper, reproduced exactly, and the
  two functions shown to invert each other.
- **rank detection** — three spreads built from two independent factors must be reported as
  fewer than three bets, and two identical spreads as one.
- **sizing monotonicity** — doubling the estimated deviation at least halves the size, and
  no input produces a size that is negative, infinite, or not a number.
- **dependency direction** — Step 1 and Step 2 must not import Step 3. Checked by parsing
  the syntax tree rather than grepping, because those files mention Step 3 in prose all
  over and a grep would report dependencies that do not exist.

**Then the suite was mutation-tested.** Fifteen plausible defects were introduced one at a
time — an uncertainty haircut applied upwards, a dropped edge requirement, growth-optimal
leverage dividing by the deviation instead of the variance, a kill switch reporting the
last breach instead of the first — and the suite was run against each.

Thirteen were caught immediately. **Two were missed, and both were failures of the fixtures
rather than of the checks.** The kill-switch fixture had only one drawdown breach, so
"first" and "last" were the same answer and the check could not tell them apart. The
threshold-floor fixture had no thin-but-profitable row, so the minimum trade count could be
deleted without changing the result. Both fixtures were rebuilt to discriminate; all
fifteen mutations are now caught.

A suite that has never been shown to fail is not evidence.

## Changes to existing code

Three, all to remove a second copy of something:

- `backtest.py` gained public `carry_per_night_bps` and `round_trip_bps`, so anything
  predicting what a trade will cost charges what the engine charges. `thresholds.py` was
  briefly computing its own round trip, and it disagreed: the engine weights the legs by
  the hedge ratio and the cost model does not, so the two directions financed identically
  when they should not have.
- `hedge.py`'s `net_exposure` moved from a method to a module function. The risk layer
  needs it for a relationship it has not fitted an `Estimate` for, and a second copy of
  that formula is how the screen came to admit a position that was 72% net long.
- `triallog.py` extracted the CSV append and its schema guard. Step 1 and Step 2 each had
  a copy; the copies agreed until a column was added to one of them.
