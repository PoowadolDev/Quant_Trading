`NUE~STLD` is the clearest demonstration of what deflation is for. Its probabilistic Sharpe
is **80.5%** — on its own the ratio reads as a reasonable result. Charged for the seventy-eight

best of seventy-eight trials reaches 0.264 on noise alone; this reached 0.085.

`RSG~WM` and `KEY~ZION` fail a second way. Their probability of backtest overfitting is 77%
and 67%: across the CSCV splits, the configuration that wins in sample lands in the bottom
half out of sample two times in three. Whatever those sweeps selected, they selected noise.

## Three defects the scripts found in themselves

### The trial count was wrong by a factor of nineteen

`count_trials` swept every CSV in the log directory and returned **1,464**. The real figure
is **78**.

`screen.py --dump` writes one row per tested pair into the same directory, so a single screen
of 1,208 equity pairs was being counted as 1,208 separate trials. A trial log carries a `run`
or `trial` column; a dump carries neither, and that is now the test.

`screens.csv` and `cointegration.csv` are also excluded, deliberately. They record statistical
tests rather than strategy configurations. Screening 1,192 pairs is 1,192 chances to reject a
null — which is what `multiple_testing.py` corrects for — and not 1,192 Sharpe ratios anybody
could have selected. Charging one correction with the other's tests inflates the benchmark
with things that were never candidates.

### A flag that could not do what it claimed

`purged_cv.py` offered `--plant-leak`, described as copying a future bar into the feature so
that plain k-fold would be fooled and purging would not. Run on `NUE~STLD` it reported:

```
THE PLANTED LEAK WAS NOT CAUGHT. Purging is not removing the overlap it is
supposed to remove; fix this script before believing anything it says.
```

It was right about itself, and the fault was in the flag rather than in purging. The leak it
planted was *inside each row* — feature and label contaminated together. Purging removes
*cross-fold* overlap: training rows whose outcome window reaches into the test period. No
amount of purging can fix a row that already contains its own answer.

The flag is gone. The demonstration that purging works moved into `verify_validation.py`,
on synthetic data where the overlap is constructed and the right answer is known.

### Purging is usually a no-op, and the script now says so

With a twenty-bar horizon and six folds over seven thousand bars, purging removes **0.6%** of
the training rows. A score computed on 5,990 rows is indistinguishable from one computed on
6,023, and reporting "no leakage" on that basis claims a test that never happened.

The share removed is now printed, and below 5% the verdict is `NOT TESTED` rather than
`NO LEAKAGE`:

> At that share the comparison below cannot tell a clean strategy from a leaky one.

Purging bites when the outcome window is long relative to the fold. At a 400-bar horizon with
eight folds it removes 11–13%, which is enough for the comparison to mean something.

## Two checks that asserted guesses

Both failures on the first run of the extended suite were my expectations, not the code.

**CSCV median log-odds on noise came out −0.693 where I had asserted "near zero".** That is
not a bug — it is the effect the method exists to detect. Selection on noise does not give a
*random* out-of-sample rank; it gives a *worse than random* one, because the in-sample winner
is whichever configuration overfitted the training half hardest. The check now asserts the
log-odds are not positive, and that a real edge ranks above noise.

**Purging removed 20% of training rows where I had asserted more than 25%.** Ten folds of 200
rows with a 200-bar horizon drops roughly one horizon either side of each test fold: about
400 of 2,000. That is derivable, and the quarter was a guess. The check now asserts the
derived range.

A suite whose thresholds are guesses fails for the wrong reasons, and passing it means less
than it appears to.
